**Translation:** What I want to point out is that an “operating method” or a “textbook sequence” is not necessarily the truth. A technical limit? I will accept that along a one-dimensional, straight-line path, but it is not future-oriented. I know tokens have limits. Has another method—compression—been tried: compressing a thousand-line text until it is not a thousand lines of data but one piece of information?

## The problem

Naive truncation can discard lines without preserving their dependencies. Yet some long histories are generated by a smaller structure: invariants, dependencies, corrections, exceptions, and state transitions. A useful compression should retain enough of that generator to reproduce correct behavior on cases not copied into the packet.

This is not a claim that every thousand lines fit losslessly into one sentence. Rare exceptions may be less compressible and more important than the dominant pattern.

## Proposed test

Generate train and development histories from multiple latent-program families containing goals, rule changes, retired instructions, rare exceptions, and noise. Freeze every compressor and schema before evaluation on held-out program families and an independently adjudicated real-history set. Every method receives only raw history—never the latent program, gold ledger, or test questions—and receives equal storage and inference-compute budgets. Compare oldest-first deletion, recency retention, extractive summaries, ordinary abstractive summaries, and a structured compressor with exception and provenance ledgers. Report end-to-end ledger extraction error.

Before evaluation, preregister the task distribution, category weights, the cost matrix defining downstream regret, probability elicitation, and a proper calibration score. Test exact facts, hidden decisions, rare exceptions, temporal updates, counterfactuals, and attribution across several storage budgets. Report the Pareto frontier between decision error, exact-fact and exception retention, unsupported reconstruction, calibration, and retained tokens rather than selecting a favorable aggregate after seeing results.

The structural method is supported only if it improves the preregistered Pareto trade-off on held-out program families and the independently adjudicated set under equal budgets. It fails where ordinary summaries match it or “regeneration” produces fluent but incorrect details. This track tests compression efficiency under shrinking budgets, not a second independent claim that structured memory is useful.

## Claim boundary

Compression cannot create absent information or guarantee lossless recovery of incompressible records. “One structure” means a measured decision-generating representation, not storage beyond token limits.
